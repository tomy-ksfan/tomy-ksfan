# Toy Car Digital Twin

An exploratory simulation project connecting **state estimation, uncertainty quantification, and sequential decision-making** in a vehicle lane-keeping example.

## Project overview

The car aims to remain near the center of a straight lane while maintaining a target speed. Its observations are noisy, and road conditions are only partially known and may change over time. The digital twin maintains an evolving representation of the car and its environment, using incoming observations to support subsequent steering and acceleration decisions.

The project examines how different estimation and control approaches behave within the same feedback loop. The emphasis is on understanding the relationship between what the system observes, what it estimates, and how it acts. All observations and vehicle responses are generated by a simplified simulator.

## State and action model

The hidden state and control input are represented by

$$
S_t = (y_t,\psi_t,v_t,\mu_t)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
U_t = (\delta_t,a_t)^{\mathsf T}.
$$

| Component | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| $y_t$ | Lateral displacement from the lane center. |
| $\psi_t$ | Heading angle relative to the lane direction. |
| $v_t$ | Vehicle speed. |
| $\mu_t$ | Unknown road-friction condition. |
| $\delta_t$ | Steering command. |
| $a_t$ | Acceleration or braking command. |

At a general level, the state evolves according to

$$
S_{t+1}=\mathcal{F}(S_t,U_t,\varepsilon_t),
$$

where $\varepsilon_t$ represents process disturbances. Steering changes heading, which subsequently affects lateral position. Acceleration and braking change speed, while road friction influences the response to both commands. Noisy observations provide information about motion and road conditions; the controller does not receive the simulated true state.

The dynamics are intentionally simplified to support controlled comparisons. They are not a calibrated vehicle model.

## Estimation and decision methods

The estimation component compares three representations of the information available to the controller:

| Estimation approach | Role in the project |
| --- | --- |
| Direct observations with a Bayesian friction belief | Uses current sensor readings for vehicle motion and maintains a separate probability distribution over friction. |
| Extended Kalman filter (EKF) with a friction belief | Combines a nonlinear motion forecast with new observations, while updating friction separately. |
| Ensemble Kalman filter (EnKF) | Estimates vehicle motion and friction jointly through an ensemble of possible states. |

Action selection is studied using **rule-based control**, **model predictive control (MPC) with a friction belief**, and **ensemble-based MPC**. The rule-based controller responds to current estimates. MPC evaluates predicted outcomes over a short horizon, applies the first selected action, and plans again after receiving new observations. The ensemble approach represents uncertainty across possible states when comparing actions.

The figures include two ensemble-MPC settings labeled `lambda=0` and `lambda=1`. These indicate the absence or presence of an additional penalty on variability in predicted cost. Detailed objective functions and tuning choices are outside this public overview.

An offline Kalman smoothing analysis is also included in the broader project. It uses later observations to reassess earlier state estimates and is kept separate from online decision-making.

## Simulation and evaluation

Each simulation repeatedly selects an action, advances the vehicle, generates observations, and updates the digital twin. The simulated truth is retained separately for evaluation. Comparisons identify both the estimator and the controller because changing either can affect the observed behavior.

The evaluation considers lane keeping, speed tracking, estimation error, uncertainty intervals, control effort, and computation time. The main example examines an abrupt change in road friction. Additional short checks explore different friction levels, noisier observations, and mismatch between the physical and digital models.

The displayed example illustrates several tradeoffs:

- Predictive controllers maintain speeds closer to the target than the prescribed rule-based baseline, which brakes throughout this run. The comparison does not establish superiority over a separately tuned rule-based controller.
- Both ensemble-MPC settings remain within the lane in this example, while the EKF with grid MPC briefly crosses the boundary.
- The ensemble setting with the additional variability penalty has the lowest realized cost in the displayed run. A small repeated-run check does not establish a general ranking.
- State estimates track the observed motion, while friction uncertainty remains broad. The shaded intervals summarize uncertainty and do not establish calibration from this example alone.

These are illustrative simulation findings. Their interpretation depends on the model, scenario, and selected settings.
